# Premium Web Design

Use this when building or restyling a website or landing page and the user wants it to look
polished and intentional.

## Before any code: choose a direction

Pick one direction and state it in one sentence, e.g. "quiet luxury: lots of white space,
serif headlines, one deep accent color." Base it on who the site is for. Don't mix
directions.

## Things that make a page look cheap (avoid)

- Purple-to-blue gradients, glowing blobs, and emoji used as icons.
- Every section centered with the same card grid.
- Five font sizes that are all close together.
- Drop shadows on everything; borders *and* shadows on the same card.
- Stock phrases like "Unlock the power of…" or "Seamless experience".

## Things that make it look expensive (do)

- **Type:** one display face and one text face at most. Big contrast between headline and
  body sizes (e.g. 56px vs 18px). Tight letter-spacing on large headlines, comfortable line
  height (1.5–1.7) on body text, and lines no longer than about 70 characters.
- **Spacing:** use a scale (4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, 48, 64, 96, 128) and be generous. Space
  between sections should be clearly bigger than space inside them.
- **Color:** a near-neutral base, one accent used sparingly for actions. Check contrast is
  readable (4.5:1 for body text).
- **Layout:** vary section layouts; use asymmetry and alignment to a grid. Let one element
  per section be the hero.
- **Detail:** consistent corner radius, subtle 1px borders, real images or none at all,
  hover and focus states on everything clickable.
- **Motion:** small and quick (150–250ms), only to explain changes. Respect "reduce motion".

## Finish

Review the page at phone width and desktop width. Fix anything that wraps badly, overflows,
or has tap targets smaller than 44px. Then list the 3 changes that made the most difference.
